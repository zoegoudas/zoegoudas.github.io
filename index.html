Speech feedback device for deaf learners

Read moreShow less

A wearable collar that gives deaf and hard-of-hearing learners feedback on their speech. Their voices work, but they can't hear their own mistakes to fix them. The device stands in for that missing feedback by sensing how the throat and mouth move, not just how the speech sounds.

The design pairs throat and nasal accelerometers and surface EMG sensors under the jaw with a phone app. A small processor on the collar streams the signals over Bluetooth, and the app scores each attempt on pitch, voicing and nasality against a target, then shows the learner what to change.

2026 to present

Methods

Acellular allograft tissue engineering

Read moreShow less

An evidence-generation plan for a hypothetical acellular dermal allograft on backorder: could a modified tissue process raise production without hurting quality?

We designed three blinded, statistically powered studies, each with clear acceptance criteria for management: Instron tensile testing for mechanical integrity, ELISA for collagen and elastin content, and an in vitro fibroblast repopulation assay with DAPI/phalloidin staining and DNA quantification to confirm decellularization.

Sound frequency and radish seed germination

Read moreShow less

An independent experiment on whether audible sound changes germination time in radish (Raphanus sativus). I played 70 Hz, 300 Hz and 2000 Hz sine tones on a 6-hour cycle against a silent control, holding light, temperature and volume constant, and tracked radicle emergence every 6 hours.

70 Hz cut mean germination time by about 20% (21.6 vs. 27 hours, p = 0.010), 2000 Hz delayed it (30.6 hours, p = 0.006), and 300 Hz had no significant effect. Written up as a 4,000-word IB Extended Essay.

Jan to May 2024

Methods
Experimental design, t-tests, scientific writing

Background noise and C. elegans chemotaxis

Read moreShow less

Tested how brown, pink, white and blue noise affect C. elegans chemotaxis toward NaCl. I cultured worms on E. coli-seeded NGM plates, ran chemotaxis assays under five sound conditions and compared chemotaxis indices with a one-way ANOVA.

No condition differed significantly (p = 0.708), though the data hinted at a link between a noise's frequency profile and the worms' preference. Presented at two regional science fairs and placed 2nd at the Jersey Shore Science Fair.
