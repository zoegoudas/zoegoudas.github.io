Probiotic Hydrogel for Ulcerative Colitis

Jan – Apr 2026

Background

Ulcerative colitis is a form of inflammatory bowel disease that affects about 900,000 Americans. The immune system attacks the lining of the colon, causing pain, bleeding, fatigue, and a higher risk of colon cancer.

Treatment is mostly reactive. Steroids such as prednisone have many side effects, more than half of patients on biologic drugs don't reach remission, and about 30% of patients end up needing surgery. There is no preventive therapy.

Proposed Design

My group and I proposed a hydrogel that can be sprayed onto the inside of the colon. It carries E. coli engineered to produce interleukin-10 (IL-10), a protein that calms inflammation.

IL-10 has been given intravenously at high doses for inflammatory bowel disease, but it can’t be taken orally because stomach acid (pH 1.5–3.5) breaks it down. Our solution is to have E. coli produce IL-10 in the colon itself, avoiding the stomach and releasing it where it’s needed. E. coli naturally lives in the gut, the engineered strain is non-pathogenic, and because the bacteria keep growing, one application could treat the colon for longer than a standard medication.

Hypothesis

The hydrogel will reduce inflammation by delivering IL-10 directly to the inflamed tissue, while being safe for the surrounding cells and reliably adhering to the intestinal mucus layer.

Testing Methods

To measure porosity, we would use atomic force microscopy and STED microscopy to find pore size and void fraction, then track how quickly IL-10 diffuses through gels with different crosslink densities.

For biocompatibility, we would run a direct-contact cytotoxicity test following ISO 10993-5 on normal human colon cells (NCM460), checking cell viability every 24 hours for 3 days against positive and negative controls.

To test mucus adhesion, we would coat a surface with simulated intestinal mucus, apply the gel, and expose it to fluid flow that mimics the gut, measuring how strongly it sticks and how long it stays.

Finally, we would test it in humanized mice (CD34+ NSG-SGM3-IL15) with colitis induced by dextran sulfate sodium. One limitation is that the gel would be injected in mice rather than sprayed during an endoscopy.
