About

I’m a senior biomedical engineering major with a math minor in the Honors College at Rutgers University–New Brunswick, graduating in May 2027. I’m interested in tissue engineering, biomaterials, medical devices, biosensors, wearable health tech, drug delivery and neural engineering.

For my senior capstone, my team is designing a low-cost obstetric training simulator with the Rutgers-Newark Midwifery Nursing program. I’ve also worked and volunteered as an EMT for four years, and that time with patients shapes how I approach engineering.

I’m looking for R&D internships for spring/summer 2027 and plan to pursue a master’s in BME.

Outside of school, I play guitar, grow plants, hike, snowboard and train calisthenics.

EMS

  • Volunteer EMT, Morganville Emergency Medical ServicesMorganville, NJ · Oct 2022 – Present
  • EMT, Quality Medical TransportBarnegat, NJ · Jan 2025 – Present

Certifications · NJ EMT · CPR (AHA) · Bloodborne Pathogens · HIPAA · ImageTrend ePCR · CEVO IV · ICS 100, 200, 700 and 800

I joined Morganville EMS as a cadet in high school, where I found my passion for patient care, and went on to become a New Jersey certified EMT. Four years later, I still volunteer there, now leading crews and helping train new EMTs and cadets, and I work part-time as an EMT at Quality Medical Transport. Every call is a chance to make a difference in someone’s life, and that’s what keeps me going.

A shift can mean calming a patient through a panic attack or treating someone too badly injured to respond to my assessment. As a provider, I use the same medical devices I study as a biomedical engineering student, and I’ve also been the patient being assessed and treated. That lets me design with three people in mind, the provider working under pressure, the patient relying on the device, and the engineer building it. What excites me most about biomedical engineering is turning those ideas into devices that improve patients’ lives and help providers give them the best possible care.
