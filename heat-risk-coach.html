Adaptive Heat Risk Coach App

Jan – Apr 2026

Problem

During a rare spring heat wave, temperatures reached 85°F for several days but still did not meet state guidelines for switching buildings from heat to air conditioning. I have no preexisting health conditions and took steps to limit my symptoms, but I still felt the effects. The heat was likely much harder on others, especially people with a history of heat-related illness. Weather apps send everyone the same heat alert, but heat tolerance varies widely from person to person. I wanted to build an app that learns each user’s sensitivity and give advice before symptoms escalate.

Users

I designed the app for heat-sensitive people, including those with migraines or POTS, and for athletes and outdoor workers. Anyone can use it during a heat wave.

System Design

Users log temperature, humidity, time outdoors, hydration, activity level, sleep quality, and symptoms such as fatigue, headache, and dizziness. From these, the app calculates the heat index and a personal risk score and plots trends over time.

The app has three parts. The brain finds patterns between conditions and symptoms. I wrote its rules based on conversations with friends and classmates about how heat affects them. The skin is the dashboard that displays risk and trends. The coach turns those patterns into advice.

Adaptive Coaching

The coach gives three types of tips. Preventive tips come before symptoms start, such as “You're approaching your typical fatigue threshold. Drink water and eat a light snack.” Reactive tips respond to reported symptoms, such as “You reported dizziness and high heat. Move to a cooler environment immediately.” Adaptive tips use patterns over time, such as “You tend to underhydrate in the afternoon. Would you like to set a reminder?”

After each tip, the coach asks whether symptoms improved, worsened, or stayed the same, and uses the answer to tailor future advice to that user.

Future Work

Manual logging is easy to forget and can be inaccurate. Next, I plan to integrate wearables such as an Apple Watch or Fitbit to record heart rate, skin temperature, sleep, and activity automatically. Users can already export their logs to share with a doctor, and a future version could alert the doctor directly when it detects a high-risk pattern.

Reflection

I built the first version in Base44, an AI app builder, because I didn’t have the coding experience to write an app from scratch. AI helped me build the app but did little to help me come up with the idea. Its suggestions felt superficial, and the real idea came from my own experience. I then decided to develop my coding skills and rebuilt the app myself on GitHub.
